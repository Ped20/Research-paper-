## 5. The stage-stratified literature: three silos, one species

The preceding sections established that PEG screening at germination is the most widely used method for ranking Solanaceae genotypes for drought tolerance, and that its ability to predict field performance at the reproductive stage is unvalidated in *Capsicum*. This section argues that the reason is structural rather than incidental: **drought-tolerance research in pepper is stratified by developmental stage, and the strata do not communicate.**

Three largely separate bodies of work exist for *Capsicum*, each at a different stage:

| Stratum | Stage | Typical approach | Representative outputs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Germination | PEG-induced osmotic stress in Petri dishes | Ranked tolerant/susceptible genotype lists (Section 3) |
| **B** | Vegetative | Controlled water withholding, pot or greenhouse | Drought-resistant accessions identified by recovery |
| **C** | Reproductive | Field or greenhouse water stress; GWAS and QTL mapping | Yield-component loci and candidate genes |

Tomato exhibits the same stratification, but with materially more work in each stratum. Eggplant shows it too, with a fourth strategy — wild-relative introgression — that is far more developed than in either of the other two crops.

**The organising observation:** no study in any of the three crops connects all three strata. Genotypes identified as tolerant at germination are not the genotypes tested at the reproductive stage; the loci mapped in stratum C are not the loci queried in the genotypes ranked in stratum A. The literature therefore contains three partial answers to one question, with no bridge between them.

---

### 5.1 *Capsicum*: the strata

**Stratum A — germination.** The screening studies catalogued in Section 3 and Table 2 constitute this stratum. They are numerous, inexpensive, and independently conducted. Their outputs are rankings.

**Stratum B — vegetative stage.** A screen of 100 *Capsicum* accessions under highly controlled conditions identified resistant accessions — **IT308761, IT250221 and IT158637** — that maintained resistance through 21 days of water withholding, with five accessions (**IT158411, IT163497, IT225019, IT237567, IT236754**) classified susceptible on the basis of visible withering, chlorosis and leaf abscission ✅. Resistance was corroborated under greenhouse conditions, which is a meaningful advance over single-environment screening: the authors explicitly note that "evaluation of drought resistance in *Capsicum* germplasm is still lacking," and position the work as filling that gap. The susceptibility of anthocyanin-accumulating accessions was noted as a possible correlate of the *An2* MYB locus.

A separate screen of 30 *Capsicum* accessions for root architecture — first in paper pouches, then pots, then field — identified *C. baccatum* and *C. chinense* accessions as carrying the longest and thinnest root systems, with *Serrano Criollo de Morelos* showing a high proportion of thick laterals ✅. Root architecture is a plausible mechanistic link between vegetative and reproductive performance, but the study did not test it against either stratum.

**Stratum C — reproductive stage.** This is where the correction to the earlier draft matters most. A recent study combined genome-wide association in a Balkan *C. annuum* diversity panel (*n* = 133) with quantitative trait locus mapping in an interspecific backcross inbred line population (*n* = 76), phenotyping both under well-watered and water-stressed conditions for fruit yield and quality ✅. Loci on chromosomes 5 and 6 harboured candidate genes including *GRL1*, *CYP77A19* and an endoglucanase-like gene, with the authors attributing the effect to possible *cis*-regulatory variation. Critically, the paper states that the genetic architecture of **reproductive-stage drought tolerance remains poorly resolved** — which is an accurate description of the state of the field, and consistent with the argument here.

**Cross-stratum: species-level variation.** A comparison of the three most widely cultivated *Capsicum* species in Africa, under four drought regimes imposed at three developmental stages, found ***C. chinense* more drought-tolerant than *C. annuum* and *C. frutescens*** ✅. The same study reported that all three species were **more susceptible to drought at the vegetative stage than at flowering or fruiting** — stage-specificity of tolerance demonstrated within *Capsicum* itself, which is the core phenomenon this review is concerned with. Drought also altered capsaicin content in *C. chinense* but not *C. annuum*, a quality consequence of applied relevance.

**Cross-stratum: mechanism.** A comparison of drought-tolerant KCa-4884 and drought-susceptible G-4 found that **all twelve examined aquaporins were up-regulated in the tolerant genotype and down-regulated in the susceptible genotype** ✅. This is a coherent, genotype-contrasting expression signature — precisely the kind of result that a screening programme would need in order to attach a molecular marker to a phenotype. It also illustrates the gap: the aquaporin work used its own tolerant and susceptible genotypes, which were not the accessions from stratum B nor the panel members from stratum C.

**Genomic resources are not the constraint.** Pepper has a graph pan-genome and a genome variation map covering 500 accessions across five domesticated species and close wild relatives, with introgressions from *C. baccatum* into *C. chinense* and *C. frutescens* carrying genes conferring biotic and abiotic stress tolerance ✅. The genomes of CM334 and Zunla-1 provide reference assemblies. *Whatever limits progress in pepper drought breeding, it is not the availability of sequence.*

**Candidate genes in *Capsicum*.** A growing but still narrow catalogue: ***CaNAC46*** (best characterised; NAC-ATAF; validated by transgenic *Arabidopsis* and by VIGS in pepper), *CaDREBLP1* (rapidly induced by dehydration and salinity), *MYB1* and *CaDIM1* (ABA-associated), *CaSBP13* and *CaDR1* (identified through RNA-seq and GWAS), a Y1SK2-type dehydrin, *HAT5* (proline metabolism), and the aquaporin set above ✅. **Table 4 records the validation host for each.** The distinction matters: a gene demonstrated in *Arabidopsis* is not a gene demonstrated in chilli, and most reviews conflate the two.

### 5.2 Tomato: the same strata, more work in each

Tomato's advantage is not a different strategy but greater depth per stratum.

**Stratum C is where tomato diverges most sharply.** Drought-related quantitative trait loci have been mapped repeatedly: 19 from sub-near-isogenic lines of *S. habrochaites* on the centromeric end of chromosome 9, for traits including specific leaf area, shoot dry weight, fruit yield and carbon isotope discrimination; 56 from 119 F7 recombinant inbred lines, of which 11 were expressed under drought; 12 for male and female fertility and flowering characteristics under drought; and 54 from a multi-parent advanced generation intercross (MAGIC) population ✅. **CRISPR validation exists** — *SlMAPK3* mutants show enlarged stomatal apertures, elevated H₂O₂ and malondialdehyde, increased electrolyte leakage, reduced antioxidant enzyme activity, and down-regulation of *SlDHN*, *SlDREB* and *SlGST* ✅.

**Stratum A is active but recent.** A five-genotype screen under 0%, 3% and 6% PEG found significant genotype × stress interactions across germination and seedling traits, with **NGRCO9569, Monoprecos and Khumal 2** maintaining germination, vigour and biomass, while **Srijana** failed to germinate entirely at 6% PEG ✅. Multi-trait indices — MGIDI and DRI — were used to rank genotypes ✅. Two features make this study exemplary and worth citing as a methodological model: it reports the **genotype × stress interaction** rather than performance at a single stress level, and it states plainly that the tolerant genotypes are "promising candidates for field validation" — that is, the authors themselves identify stratum C validation as outstanding ✅.

**Mechanism spans the strata better than in pepper.** The *SlDREB* family has been characterised genome-wide, with transcriptome analysis identifying family members responsive to drought and heat ✅. Transcription-factor catalogues across tomato include positive regulators (*SlAREB1*, *SlAREB2*, *SlJUB1*, *SlNAC4*, *SlNAC6*, *SlNAC35*, *SlNAC042*, *SlNAP1*, *SlNAC2*, *SlWRKY8*, *SlWRKY6*, *SlERF5*, *SlERF84*, *SlMYB49*, *SlbZIP1*, HD-Zip) and — routinely omitted from reviews — **negative regulators** (*SlWRKY81*, *SlMYB50*, *SlMYB55*, *SlEREB1*, *SlbZIP38*) ✅. A transcriptome-based meta-analysis has further consolidated drought-responsive regulatory genes ✅.

**Wild relatives are used as donors** (*S. habrochaites*, *S. pimpinellifolium*), which is how the QTL-rich material entered the cultivated gene pool in the first place.

### 5.3 Eggplant: a fourth strategy, better developed

Eggplant demonstrates the strategy that both pepper and tomato under-use: **systematic exploitation of wild relatives**.

Cultivated *S. melongena* is consistently the **most drought-sensitive** genotype in comparative trials. Under water stress, eggplant showed reductions in nine agronomic traits including leaf number (12.9–15.8%), plant height (15.4–25.1%), leaf area index (47.7–55.4%) and biomass (31.4–38.6%) ✅. Wild species were markedly more tolerant: *S. macrocarpon* essentially maintained biomass, while *S. dasyphyllum* showed an intermediate response and interspecific hybrids retained partial heterosis ✅.

A screen of 24 genotypes across 15 *Solanum* species identified ***S. torvum*, *S. viarum*, *S. violaceum* and *S. aethiopicum*** as tolerant, with *S. pseudocapsicum* and *S. melongena* ranking lowest ✅. Principal component analysis identified catalase activity, proline, stomatal conductance, transpiration rate, root length and shoot dry weight as the traits driving differences, explaining **80.2%** of total variation ✅ — a useful prioritisation for anyone designing a reduced trait panel. Genetic parameters for drought tolerance have been estimated across *S. melongena*, wild relatives and their F₁ hybrids in the field, identifying *S. insanum* as a tolerant donor within the primary gene pool ✅.

Progressive-drought transcriptomics in contrasting eggplant genotypes resolved the tolerant programme as **early ABA-centred regulation** — *ABI5*, *TAS14* and LEA/dehydrin-related loci — coupled to transport and redox homeostasis, whereas the sensitive genotype showed early RNA and protein-turnover signatures instead ✅. The distinction is temporal as well as compositional, which again argues for stage- and time-resolved design rather than single-timepoint sampling.

### 5.4 Synthesis: the strata need a bridge

Three conclusions follow, and they constitute this review's central synthesis.

**First, the binding constraint is not genomic resources.** Pepper has a 500-accession pan-genome; tomato has a saturated QTL literature; eggplant has a characterised wild-relative donor pool. Sequence, markers and mapping populations are available.

**Second, the constraint is not effort.** Each crop has substantial work at each stage. The problem is that the work is *stratified*.

**Third — and this is the actionable claim — the strata are connected by nothing, and connecting them is cheap.** The genotypes that a germination screen ranks are not the genotypes tested under vegetative drought, and neither set is the panel in which loci were mapped. The consequence is that a breeder holding a ranked list from a PEG screen (Stratum A) has no route to the loci in Stratum C, and a geneticist holding a validated locus has no route to knowing whether the lines it would select for are the lines a screen would have found.

**The bridge is a shared genotype set.** Screening panels, validation trials and mapping populations should draw on common, diverse, publicly available germplasm — the pepper pan-genome accessions, the *Capsicum* core collection accessions that appear in the research programmes above (IT-series), and wild relatives of the kinds that eggplant work has shown carry tolerance. Design the panel once, and the three strata become three experiments on one population rather than three literatures about one species.

This is not a methodological innovation. It is a coordination recommendation, and its cost is close to zero relative to any single one of the experiments it connects. That is precisely why its absence is worth stating.

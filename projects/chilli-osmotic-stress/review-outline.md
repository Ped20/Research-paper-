## 1. THE THESIS — write this first, everything follows

> **Osmotic screening with PEG is the cheapest and most widely used route to identifying drought-tolerant genotypes across the Solanaceae, but three problems limit its value: PEG does not faithfully simulate drought; the screen's ability to predict field performance at the agronomically relevant stage is unvalidated — and where tested in cereals, weak or negative; and the family is markedly asymmetric in what that screening has produced. Tomato and eggplant have converted screens into mapped QTL, characterised genes and wild-relative donors, while *Capsicum* has the genomic sequence and the screening literature but not the validated loci. The transferable path forward is a standardised, stage-aware, cost-tiered pipeline.**

Three component claims, each grounded in `study-inventory.md`:

1. **The method is contested** — documented PEG artefacts (absorption, translocation, hypoxia, membrane injury exceeding soil drying)
2. **The predictive link is untested in *Capsicum*** — and where tested in cereals, negative (barley: r = −0.25 between germination rate under PEG and yield under drought)
3. **The family is asymmetric** — tomato and eggplant have the genetics; *Capsicum* has the sequence and the screens

**Component 3 is what the Solanaceae scope buys you.** A chilli-only review cannot make this argument, and it is the component that makes the review useful beyond one crop.

**Review type: critical / narrative, with a documented search.** A meta-analysis is not achievable — published screening studies use different PEG concentrations, traits and indices, and mostly report no variance measures, so no common effect size can be extracted. **State this openly in the Methods** rather than implying systematicity you cannot deliver.

- The problem: many screening studies, few validated outcomes

Covers osmotic stress simulation, in vitro screening, predictive validity, low-cost genotyping. **Excludes** salinity, heat, biotic stress except where methodologies overlap; excludes transgenic breeding strategy
- **Prior-review differentiation** (§1) — mandatory
- **Methodology of this review:** databases, search strings, date range, inclusion/exclusion, records screened → included. 150–300 words, including the explicit statement that this is a critical rather than systematic review

### 2. Osmotic stress simulation: agents, chemistry, and documented limitations ⭐

**Place this BEFORE the achievements section.** The limitations are what make the later synthesis necessary.

- **PEG chemistry and the molecular-weight problem.** PEG-6000 dominates seed screening; 3350, 4000, 8000 also used. Behaviour is MW-dependent
- **Concentration → osmotic potential.** Report the relationship; note that **published studies routinely report nominal % without verifying osmotic potential**. Michel & Kaufmann (1973) is the standard conversion reference. Note temperature dependence — incubation temperature changes the stress actually applied
- **Documented artefacts:**
  - PEG absorbed by roots, translocated to shoots, deposited on leaves; stomatal damage and **greater membrane injury than equivalent soil drying**, with slower recovery
  - Viscous PEG solutions limit oxygen diffusion → **hypoxia, a confounding stress**
  - PEG can penetrate seeds on direct contact, with toxic effects
  - Commercial PEG impurities; batch variation
  - **PEG triggers dehydration but does not accurately simulate drought** (2025)
  - Documented case of PEG producing the **opposite** effect to real drought on a biological process
- **Alternatives and their problems:** mannitol, sorbitol, sucrose (metabolisable — alter carbon supply as well as water potential); solidified PEG media and raft/membrane systems; soil-based drying
- **Ceiling-effect finding:** most tomato genotypes fail to germinate at all above −0.35 MPa. A fixed concentration either fails to discriminate or produces floor effects
- **Verdict to state explicitly:** PEG is fit for **relative ranking** under a defined artificial stress; it is **not** a mechanistic model of field drought, and papers should stop calling the treatment "drought" without qualification

### 3. Screening in the Solanaceae: what has been reported

Organise by crop — *Capsicum*, tomato, eggplant and wild relatives — so the asymmetry becomes visible as the section builds.

- **Stages used:** germination-stage dominates; seedling-stage common; callus/tissue-culture selection reported in tomato
- **Typical design:** genotype × PEG factorial, completely randomised, 3–4 reps
- **Traits:** germination %, rate, MGT, index; root and shoot length; root:shoot; seedling FW/DW; vigour index; some add proline, RWC, membrane stability
- **Indices:** GSI, SSI, STI, TOL, MP, GMP — define each, give the formula, **state when each misleads** (Table 3)
- **Grouping:** PCA + cluster analysis, dendrograms

**Table 2 — Screening studies inventory.** Full populated table in `study-inventory.md`. Columns: study · species · n · stage · PEG level · indices · genotypes identified · checks · limitations

> **Three findings to highlight:**
> - In a 22-genotype chilli study, the commercial *drought-tolerant* check (Gada MK F1) ranked only **moderately** tolerant — the authors attributed this to stage- and mechanism-specificity. **Published evidence for this review's thesis**, from within the chilli literature itself
> - A tomato callus study determined **genotype-specific LC50** PEG concentrations rather than one fixed threshold — methodologically superior, and worth promoting into the minimum standard
> - **Pseudo-replication is pervasive:** germination % analysed by ANOVA with seeds as the unit. Germination is binomial; the unit is the dish or plot. **Count how many studies do this** — a quantitative critical finding about the literature, and one you can actually report as a number

### 4. The predictive gap: does the Petri dish predict the field? ⭐⭐

**The intellectual centre and the most likely original contribution. Nobody appears to have synthesised this for the Solanaceae.**

- **The assumption under test:** that ranking genotypes by germination under PEG predicts field performance under drought
- **Evidence from cereals, where it has been tested:**
  - A 2024 barley study of 164 lines, designed explicitly to test "the reliability of proxy methods", found germination rate under PEG correlated **negatively (r = −0.25)** with seed yield under drought at heading
  - Germination percentage correlated **negatively** with root:shoot fresh weight under PEG (r = −0.37)
  - Some genotypes performed **better** under PEG than control — biologically implausible for a true water deficit, and itself evidence of non-drought PEG effects
- **Why the gap exists:** germination-phase osmotic tolerance and reproductive-stage drought tolerance involve different mechanisms. Tomato and chilli are typically drought-stressed at flowering and fruit set. A genotype can be tolerant at one stage and sensitive at another — exactly what the chilli check-genotype anomaly shows
- **What is missing for *Capsicum*:** no published multi-season study appears to correlate germination-stage PEG indices against field drought performance. **State this as an identified gap with a falsifiable prediction** — the R5 move

**Figure 3 — Stage-specificity of drought tolerance.** Conceptual schematic: germination, seedling and reproductive-stage tolerance as partially overlapping sets; PEG screens the first, field yield is determined by the third. **Label as schematic.**

### 5. The family asymmetry: what tomato and eggplant have that *Capsicum* does not ⭐⭐

**The section the Solanaceae scope enables. This is the analytical core of the review.**

- **Tomato:** many drought QTL — 19 from *S. habrochaites* sub-NILs (chromosome 9); 56 from 119 F7 RILs, 11 under drought; 12 for fertility/flowering under drought; 54 from a MAGIC population. Genome-wide characterisation of the *SlDREB* family. CRISPR validation (*SlMAPK3*). Wild relatives (*S. habrochaites*, *S. pimpinellifolium*) actively used as donors
- ***Capsicum*:** sequence available (CM334, Zunla-1 assemblies) but **validated drought loci are scarce**. Characterised entries are few — ***CaNAC46*** (best documented), *CaDREB1*, *CaAIMK1*. GWAS has concentrated on fruit quality and disease resistance
- **Eggplant:** wild-relative strategy **established** — *S. torvum*, *S. viarum*, *S. violaceum*, *S. aethiopicum*, *S. insanum* identified as tolerant donors; cultivars highly sensitive; progressive-drought transcriptomics resolved tolerant vs sensitive programmes (*ABI5*, *TAS14*, LEA/dehydrin)
- **The correction most reviews miss:** much *Capsicum* gene work is validated by **heterologous expression in *Arabidopsis***, not in pepper. That is a lower evidence level than it is usually reported as. **Report the validation host in every row of Table 4**

**Table 4 — Candidate genes with true evidence level.** Include negative regulators (*SlWRKY81*, *SlMYB50*, *SlMYB55*, *SlEREB1*, *SlbZIP38*) — most reviews omit them, and their absence distorts the picture

**Table 5 — The asymmetry matrix.** Tomato vs *Capsicum* vs eggplant across: drought QTL mapped · GWAS for drought · characterised TF families · CRISPR validation · wild relatives used as donors

### 6. Low-cost, scalable genotyping: options for resource-limited laboratories ⭐

**Answers the practical question directly; forms the second half of the R4 deliverable.**

| Approach | Cost | Scale | Equipment | Evidence bought |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **SSR genotyping** | Low | High | Thermal cycler + gel/capillary | Marker–trait association; validated *Capsicum* SSRs exist, including markers associated with stress tolerance index |
| **Amplicon sequencing of candidate genes** | Low–moderate | Medium–high | Cyclers + sequencing service | SNP discovery in genes you already suspect |
| **Extreme-phenotype pool sequencing** | Moderate **per library**, not per genotype | High | Sequencing service | Genome-wide allele-frequency differences from 2–4 libraries |
| **GBS / RAD-seq** | ~$10–50 per sample | High | Sequencing service | Genome-wide markers for GWAS or diversity |
| **KASP** | ~$0.05–0.10 per data point | Very high | **Standard real-time PCR instrument** | Deployment: routine screening of many lines, few markers |
| **RAPD / ISSR alone** | Very low | — | Gel documentation | ⚠️ Reproducibility problems; increasingly rejected. Not recommended as primary evidence |

- Note that **KASP runs on standard real-time PCR instruments** — the barrier is assay design, not equipment. This matters for Indian and other resource-limited laboratories
- Present the **tiered decision framework** (Figure 4): what a lab with only PCR should do; what a lab with qPCR should do; what to outsource
- **Report negative cost/benefit honestly:** SSR and amplicon approaches are cheap but discovery-limited; pool-seq and GBS buy breadth but require **bioinformatics capacity, which may be the real constraint — not money**

### 7. Synthesis: a proposed integrated framework ⭐ THE R4 DELIVERABLE

Present as **Figure 1** (pipeline with decision points) plus **Table 7** (proposed minimum reporting standard).

```
STAGE 1 — Screen
  · ≥20 genotypes INCLUDING wild relatives, not just cultivars
      ↳ the eggplant literature shows wild species carry the tolerance —
        a cultivated-only panel is a design flaw
  · Genotype-specific sub-lethal PEG level (LC50 approach), not one fixed level
  · Osmotic potential VERIFIED with an osmometer or psychrometer
  · Petri dish = experimental unit; ≥4 replicates; ≥10 seeds per dish
  · GLMM (binomial) for germination %; report the G×T interaction
  · Multiple indices; group by PCA + clustering
  · Retain a SPECTRUM (tolerant / intermediate / susceptible)
      ↳ selection truncation attenuates the correlation you will later test
  DECISION: which genotypes advance?

STAGE 2 — Validate (the step most often omitted, and the reason the
          literature has not answered the central question)
  · Managed drought at the agronomically relevant stage, not natural drought
  · Irrigated control; RCBD; ≥2 seasons; site-year as random effect
  · Report the correlation between screening index and field response
  DECISION: does the screen predict the field? If not, the screen selects
            for a different trait — say so

STAGE 3 — Characterise
  · Contrasting genotype pairs (tolerant vs susceptible)
  · Candidate-gene qRT-PCR (MIQE; validated reference genes — not assumed)
  · AND/OR amplicon sequencing of candidate genes across the panel
  · Borrow from tomato: the SlDREB / SlNAC / SlAREB catalogues are the
    starting hypothesis set for Capsicum orthologs
  DECISION: expression and/or sequence difference?

STAGE 4 — Deploy (only if Stage 3 succeeds)
  · Convert polymorphism to CAPS / KASP / SSR
  · Validate marker–trait association in a wider panel
  · Independently derived material required
```

**Table 7 — Proposed minimum reporting standard:**

| Parameter | Minimum | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Genotypes | ≥20, including wild relatives and tolerant/susceptible checks | Wild species carry tolerance; checks anchor the scale |
| Osmoticum | PEG MW and supplier stated | Behaviour is MW-dependent |
| Concentration | Genotype-specific LC50, not one fixed level | Fixed levels cause floor effects (§2) |
| Osmotic potential | **Measured**, not nominal | Batch and temperature variation |
| Temperature | Stated and controlled | Osmotic potential is temperature-dependent |
| Experimental unit | Petri dish (or plot), stated explicitly | Prevents pseudo-replication |
| Replication | ≥4 units, ≥10 seeds per unit | GLMM stability |
| Statistical model | GLMM binomial for germination % | Percentages are not normal |
| Interaction | G × treatment reported | Separates tolerance from general vigour |
| Indices | ≥2 reported, with justification | Single indices mislead |
| Selection range | Spectrum retained, not extremes | Prevents correlation attenuation |
| Field validation | Multi-season, managed drought | The only test of predictive value |

### 8. Future directions

- A multi-season study correlating germination-stage PEG indices against field drought response in *Capsicum* — the single most valuable missing experiment, and **the one a reviewer will most want to see proposed**
- PEG vs. soil-drying comparison in chilli: **if the two do not rank genotypes identically, the PEG literature's rankings are not transferable**
- Drought-focused GWAS in *Capsicum* using diverse panels including wild relatives — where the crop lags tomato most
- Testing tomato's validated drought loci (*SlDREB*, *SlAREB*, *SlNAC* orthologs) in pepper
- VIGS-based functional testing in chilli itself, rather than in *Arabidopsis*
- Including wild *Capsicum* species in screening panels — the eggplant precedent suggests the tolerance is there and is not being looked for

### 9. Conclusion

- Restate the thesis: PEG screening is cheap, useful, and **oversold** — and the family's central question remains untested in *Capsicum*
- State what would change the conclusion: a multi-season chilli study showing strong screen-to-field correlation would vindicate the approach and make the existing literature far more valuable than it currently is

## 6. INTEGRITY CONSTRAINTS

You said you would use "hypothetical genotypes and no real varieties." The precise line:

| ✅ Legitimate | ❌ Not legitimate |
| --- | --- |
| Tabulating how many genotypes each **real, cited** study used | Reporting results from genotypes never tested |
| Proposing a **recommended** panel size for future work | Presenting recommendations as findings |
| Generic placeholders (`Genotype A/B`) in a schematic **labelled as schematic** | A "representative genotypes" table that is not real and cited |
| A worked example labelled `illustrative example` | Presenting modelled values as experimental results |
| Citing real tolerant lines from real studies (UARChH 42, BD-10906, LE 18, *S. torvum*) | Attributing findings to the wrong paper |

**The test:** could a reader verify this against the source? If not, it must be labelled conceptual.

**Also required:** verify every citation against the source. Frontiers runs plagiarism and reference checks, and hallucinated citations trigger desk rejection at several publishers.
